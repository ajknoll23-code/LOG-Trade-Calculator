How values are calculated · recalibrated 2026-08-13

Values get actively maintained, not set once and left alone — recalibrated as recently as today against real market data. If a number looks different than last time you checked, that's ongoing tuning, not a glitch.

Every asset gets a score from three things: how much the position scores under this league's rules, where the player sits in their age curve, and how secure their role is.

Age curve

Each position peaks and declines at a different age, based on published research rather than guesswork. A few things worth knowing: a strong current season can still meaningfully offset age — someone past their prime who's clearly still producing isn't automatically written off. QB ages differently than the rest — value drops fairly quickly after the prime years, then levels off, instead of fading gradually. Young, already-proven running backs (25 or under, genuinely producing at a real, verified level) get a further boost on top — real dynasty markets pay a premium for a back who's already shown it AND has more of a short RB career still ahead, not just "no penalty for being young." This one's specific to RB rather than every position — a RB's prime is short and every remaining year is scarce in a way that isn't equally true for, say, a quarterback.

Role tier

Elite → Every-down starter → Starter → Rotational → Understudy → Depth → Speculative. When real season stats exist they're used instead of a tier guess — but a thin or unlucky sample (a rookie buried on the bench, a season cut short) can't drag a proven talent's number below what a fair guess would already say.

Injury status

Shown next to each player's name for information — not yet factored into value. There's no reliable public data on exactly how much a given injury tag should move a number, so this stays flag-only until there is.

Draft picks

Valued against real market data rather than a flat "value drops X% per round" assumption — early picks hold their value better round-to-round than most rule-of-thumb calculators assume. Future-year picks are discounted for uncertainty.

This is a model, not a market price — use it to sanity-check trades, not as gospel. The small colored dot next to a player's name (hold/hover for detail) shows how well-evidenced that specific number is — green means it's checked against real market data, amber or red means part of it is a reasoned estimate rather than something externally verified. Not every value deserves equal trust, and this makes that visible instead of hiding it.
